First‑generation graduates earn less in the years after university.
Longitudinal research following young people into their mid‑20s shows that graduates whose parents did not attend university earn around £6,000 less per year on average than graduates with at least one graduate parent. This earnings gap remains even after accounting for subject studied, institution type, prior attainment and background, suggesting that family experience of higher education continues to shape outcomes beyond graduation.
Source: UCL Centre for Longitudinal Studies / Nuffield Foundation, ‘First in family’: higher education choices and labour market outcomes (2021).
